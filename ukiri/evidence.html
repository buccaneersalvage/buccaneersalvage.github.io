Primary Contradictions & Case Intel

Contradiction 1

USPS overnight delivery vs. “never received in the mail”

My mother sent a $120,000 cashier’s check dated December 8, 2021 by USPS Priority Overnight to Jossy’s Auto. Carrier delivery records confirm receipt on December 9, 2021, signed for by Michael Devol (identified on the shop page). The FBI holds the shipping and delivery proof.

In his August 2025 defense-investigator interview, Ukiri insisted he “never received any bank check in the mail… only handed by Yusuf.” That statement collides head-on with the USPS overnight delivery record.

Contradiction 2

Zero cars on the Copart subpoena

A defense subpoena served on Copart returned no records of the vehicle purchases Ukiri claimed under his account for “Yusuf.” His story of buying three high-end export cars and merely getting “reimbursed” has zero support in Copart’s records. (Early checks also looked at other auction platforms; Copart itself shows zero cars matching his alibi.)

BSA / DIVS summary

20 SARs, 14 CTRs, and 55 travel records

The FBI DIVS / Serial 17 financial intelligence summary on Ukiri documents (note: not “50 SARs” alone): 20 Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) filed by financial institutions, 14 Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs) on large cash transactions, and 55 travel records. Together, that equals 50+ institutional records (20 + 14 + 55) in the federal file.

PNC Bank froze and closed related accounts after the $120,000 check cleared, and Ukiri opened new accounts elsewhere afterward. Banks flagged the activity repeatedly; federal prosecutors simply never charged the shop.

Same-day timing

December 29, 2022: Ukiri filed a harassment report with the Henry County Police Department (CAD 819462, Sheriff Haskett).

December 29–30, 2022 (the same 24-hour window): the romance scammer posing as “Austin S Miller” (genaustinsmiller219@gmail.com) messaged my mother demanding she make me stop posting online. That message is preserved in sworn filings (my IC3 complaint and my FBI FD-302), not reconstructed from memory.

Sworn testimony

FBI case agent on who took the money

At the March 19, 2025 suppression hearing, FBI Special Agent Ivan Torres testified in substance that he believed Ukiri (or Jossy’s Autos) took the money, while I faced federal charges in a separate case (see July 11, 2024 on the Timeline). I allege that my prosecution followed my public complaints about FBI inaction (a retaliation theory, distinct from the criminal charge itself). Ukiri was not arrested.

Additional accounts

Second payor path into Jossy’s Auto accounts 8004 and 8012

The same FBI interview report (FD-302, dated July 13, 2023) that recorded Ukiri’s “Yusuf” story also documents a second payor path into additional Jossy’s Auto business accounts ending 8004 and 8012 (alongside the main business account ending 6004). The second payor’s name is redacted in discovery, so it is not independently identified on this public site. Working notes place that path in the six-figure range (~$193k–$198k, not locked by full bank production; one note also references a smaller single transaction).

Ukiri told agents he was unaware of accounts 8004 and 8012 and did not know that payor. The same FD-302 also notes a separate law-enforcement inquiry involving a woman who sent money to Jossy’s Auto after a debt-consolidation or magazine-ad pitch, plus an Indiana BMV senior inspector’s prior warning to Ukiri over vehicle-export scalping and scouting.

Unanswered flags

The FBI held the SARs and second-account records, yet declined to charge the shop

Federal records already contained 20 SARs, 14 CTRs, and 55 travel records (50+ flags combined, not fifty SARs), plus two separate money trails into Jossy’s Auto bank accounts (my mother’s $120,000 check and the 8004/8012 account path). Even so, agents accepted Ukiri’s “Yusuf handed me the check / I don’t know those accounts” explanation.

Agents treated him as a peripheral “victim” of Yusuf, marked the fraud referral not of interest for charges against Ukiri (per SA Torres at the suppression hearing), and, after I complained publicly about that inaction, prosecuted me while Ukiri walked free.

Note for other victims and families: If your relative’s money went to Jossy’s Auto and an agency tells you there is nothing there, ask specifically about the BSA/DIVS file, business accounts ending 8004 and 8012, and the July 13, 2023 second-payor FD-302.

Recorded statements vs. documented evidence

In the interview, Ukiri presented himself as an unwitting helper and victim. Each key claim below can be checked directly against the paper record:

  • “Yusuf… needed someone to pay for [cars at Copart] with a business check… handed him a bank check in person.”
  • Insisted that he “never received any bank check in the mail.”
  • Said the FBI was “accusing him of money laundering… cooperated fully… downloaded cell phone contents.”
  • On the foundation: “only $1600 entered… mostly his own… school supplies/clothing… suitcase for next trip.”
  • Claimed personal cost: “$85k+ lost income… no ill will… wishes family made whole.”

Where those statements break against the record

  • USPS Priority Overnight delivery + Michael Devol’s signature contradict “never received any bank check in the mail.”
  • Zero Copart vehicle purchases under subpoena contradict the “reimbursement for cars” story.
  • Ukiri’s Dec 29, 2022 police report and the scammer’s same-window silence demand contradict a passive bystander story.
  • Second-payor deposits into Jossy’s Auto accounts 8004 and 8012 sit alongside his claim that he knew nothing about those accounts.
